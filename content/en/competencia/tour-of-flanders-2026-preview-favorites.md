## Three men, 16 walls, one cobblestone at the summit

The 2026 Tour of Flanders takes place on Sunday, April 5, carrying a question that has gone unanswered for three years: who rules the Flemish cobbles? Tadej Pogačar won in 2023 and 2025. Mathieu van der Poel answered in 2024 to claim his third. Wout van Aert, the perennial third man, has five podiums in the Ronde but has never stood on the top step. The 110th edition promises to settle, at least temporarily, a debate that defines the golden age of the classics.

The 271 kilometres between Antwerp and Oudenaarde include six flat cobbled sections and 16 hellingen, the short, steep cobbled climbs that make this race the hardest on the spring calendar. The organisers have removed the Steenbeekdries and Stationsberg from the route compared to previous editions, further concentrating the action on the final circuit between the Koppenberg and the Paterberg. From the first ascent of the Oude Kwaremont, almost exactly at the halfway point, to the finish line in Oudenaarde, there are 135 kilometres of progressive attrition where each wall eliminates pretenders.

## The route: from Antwerp to the Flemish Ardennes

The race departs from Antwerp's Grote Markt heading south, crossing six Tour Villages that set the rhythm of the first half: Sint-Niklaas, Hamme, Erpe-Mere, Haaltert, Herzele and Zottegem. These are kilometres of wide, flat roads where teams control the pace and where North Sea crosswinds can split the peloton into echelons without warning. The first flat cobbled sectors appear on Lippenhovestraat and Paddestraat, near Zottegem, as a preview of what lies ahead.

The first ascent of the Oude Kwaremont marks the real start of the battle. At 2,200 metres long, 92 metres of elevation gain, an average gradient of 4% and maximum ramps of 11.6%, it is the longest cobbled climb on the course. The first 600 metres are asphalt; the remaining 1,600 metres are irregular cobblestones where mechanical and physical wear accumulates with each repetition. In the 2025 edition, Pogačar shattered the Strava KOM on this climb with a time of 2 minutes and 49 seconds, the fastest ever recorded.

After the first pass over the Kwaremont, the route winds through the Eikenberg and the Holleweg cobbles before reaching the Wolvenberg. Each of these segments functions as a filter: riders with lower short-duration power output lose positions, and teams with fewer tactical resources lose control of the group.

## The decisive circuit: Koppenberg, Taaienberg, Kwaremont and Paterberg

With 45 kilometres remaining, the race enters its terminal phase on the Koppenberg, the most feared wall on the Flemish calendar. Its 600 metres of length are deceptive: the average gradient is 11.6% and the maximum ramps reach 22% over uneven 19th-century cobblestones. The gradient rises from 12% to 20% in the first 200 metres, with 400 metres still ahead. In its first appearance at the Tour of Flanders in 1976, only four riders managed to crest it without putting a foot down. Van der Poel has produced 514 watts (6.85 W/kg) over 3 minutes and 18 seconds to climb it in race conditions, with peaks of 620 watts on the steepest section.

After the Koppenberg come the Mariaborrestraat, Taaienberg and Oude Kruisberg/Hotond, three obstacles that maintain pressure on an ever-shrinking peloton. But the race is decided on the final pairing: Oude Kwaremont and Paterberg, separated by just a few kilometres of false flat.

The Paterberg is short and brutal. At 360 metres long, 45 metres of elevation gain, an average gradient of 12.5% and maximum ramps of 20% on cobblestones, it is the last wall before the 13 flat kilometres to Oudenaarde. Van der Poel has recorded 679 watts for one minute to crest it. There is nowhere to hide: anyone who reaches the Paterberg without legs gets nailed to the cobbles.

## Pogačar: the world champion who collects Monuments

Tadej Pogačar arrives at the 2026 Tour of Flanders as defending champion and overwhelming favourite after a spring that borders on the surreal. He won Strade Bianche for the fourth time with a long-range attack over the white roads of Tuscany. Two weeks later, he conquered his first Milan-San Remo after surviving a crash 32 kilometres from the finish, attacking on the Cipressa, dropping Van der Poel on the Poggio and outsprinting Tom Pidcock by half a tyre width on the Via Roma. He has 11 Monument victories at age 27. Only Paris-Roubaix is missing from his collection.

At the 2025 Tour of Flanders, Pogačar demonstrated that his ability on the Flemish walls matches the best specialists in history. His attack on the final ascent of the Oude Kwaremont, with 19 kilometres remaining, was an exercise in raw power: approximately 519 watts for nearly three minutes to set the fastest time ever recorded on that climb. He opened a 12-second gap at the summit, doubled it on the Paterberg and finished over a minute ahead of Mads Pedersen, who beat Van der Poel in the sprint for second place.

Pogačar's pattern in the classics is predictable and yet unstoppable. He attacks repeatedly on the longer climbs, where his power-to-weight ratio makes the difference, and uses the descents to consolidate his advantage. In 2023, his first Flanders victory, the script was identical: acceleration on the Kwaremont, confirmation on the Paterberg, solo to the finish. The question for his rivals is not whether he will attack, but whether they have the legs to respond.

## Van der Poel: the puncheur chasing the all-time record

Mathieu van der Poel has won the Tour of Flanders in 2020, 2022 and 2024. With three victories, he already ties Achiel Buysse, Fiorenzo Magni, Eric Leman, Johan Museeuw, Tom Boonen and Fabian Cancellara as the most successful riders in the race's history. If he wins on Sunday, Van der Poel would be the first to reach four victories in the modern history of the Ronde. His record in the race is historic in its own right: six consecutive podiums between 2020 and 2025, an all-time record.

The warning signal for his rivals came at the E3 Saxo Classic, six days before the Ronde. Van der Poel attacked on the Paterberg with more than 40 kilometres to go and went solo. Over the final 90 minutes of the race, he averaged 446 watts, the highest figure he has ever recorded over that duration. His lead exceeded one minute, but a four-man chase group reduced it to seconds in the final kilometre. Van der Poel held on by metres to complete his third consecutive E3, a demonstration of form that confirms his specific preparation for Sunday.

The Dutchman from Alpecin-Deceuninck is the most explosive rider in the peloton over one-to-three-minute efforts, exactly the duration of the Flemish walls. His ability to produce over 600 watts on the Paterberg or Koppenberg allows him to open instant gaps that others simply cannot close. However, the wear from E3 and the accumulated effort of the season could take its toll.

In 2025, Van der Poel reached the final Kwaremont alongside Pogačar, Van Aert and Pedersen, but was unable to respond when the Slovenian accelerated. The difference between the two appears to lie in second-half endurance: Pogačar maintains his power after 250 kilometres; Van der Poel loses it fractionally.

## Van Aert: the third sword who refuses to be the eternal bridesmaid

Wout van Aert has built his entire 2026 spring around one objective: winning a cobbled Monument. The Belgian from Visma-Lease a Bike suffered a broken ankle at the turn of the year, was trapped by snow at altitude camp, missed Omloop Het Nieuwsblad through illness and had a mechanical at Le Samyn. Despite everything, he finished third at Milan-San Remo after crashing alongside Pogačar before the Cipressa and fighting back from the tail of the peloton.

At In Flanders Fields (formerly Gent-Wevelgem), Van Aert formed a leading duo with Van der Poel after attacking on the Kemmelberg with 58 kilometres remaining. The pair collaborated for dozens of kilometres but were caught near the finish, with Jasper Philipsen taking the bunch sprint. The result was disappointing, but the tactical reading is positive: Van Aert showed he has the power to go to the front at the key moments and that his endurance over long distances has improved compared to previous seasons.

Van Aert knows every metre of the Flemish roads. He grew up in Herentals, was forged in Belgian cyclocross and has been a protagonist at the Tour of Flanders since 2019. His historical problem in this race is that he shares a profile with Van der Poel and Pogačar, and in recent editions he has failed to make the difference when the other two accelerated. If he wants to win on Sunday, he needs the E3 fatigue to catch up with Van der Poel and Pogačar to make a tactical error — an unlikely scenario but not an impossible one.

## The outsiders: Pidcock, Pedersen and the team factor

Tom Pidcock arrives as a name to watch after his second place at Milan-San Remo. The Briton from Pinarello-Q36.5 is an Olympic mountain bike champion with the explosiveness needed for the short walls. His problem is that he often reaches the final 30 kilometres of cobbled Monuments without teammates, forcing him to sit on wheels and wait for a reduced sprint rather than attack.

Mads Pedersen was second at the 2025 Tour of Flanders, beating Van der Poel in the chase group sprint. The Dane from Lidl-Trek has the power and weight to survive the walls, and his sprint from small groups is one of the fastest in the peloton. If the race breaks late and a group of four or five arrives together in Oudenaarde, Pedersen is dangerous.

Team tactics will be crucial. UAE Team Emirates-XRG can surround Pogačar with luxury domestiques like McNulty, Del Toro and Wellens to control the race. Alpecin-Deceuninck relies almost exclusively on Van der Poel, with Philipsen as an alternative option if the race comes together in a group. Visma-Lease a Bike has Van Aert, plus Laporte and Kooij as wildcards.

## Sunday's tactical keys

The removal of the Steenbeekdries and Stationsberg from the 2026 route slightly reduces the number of obstacles in the final phase, which could favour one-day specialists who prefer a more concentrated finale. With fewer intermediate walls, the Koppenberg-Taaienberg-Kruisberg-Kwaremont-Paterberg block becomes an uninterrupted 25-kilometre sequence where the entire race is compressed.

Wind is the unpredictable factor. Flemish roads are flat and exposed in the first half of the course, and strong crosswinds can form echelons that leave favourites badly positioned before reaching the walls. In 2024, an early echelon complicated the race for several teams and benefited a well-organised Alpecin-Deceuninck at the head of the peloton.

The final question is how many times you can attack the Kwaremont without paying for it on the Paterberg. Pogačar has proved he can drop everyone on the third ascent of the longest wall in Flanders. Van der Poel prefers to wait for the Paterberg, where his short-term explosiveness peaks. Van Aert needs the other two to neutralise each other for space. Three strategies, 16 walls, and 271 kilometres to execute them. On Sunday, the Flemish cobbles will have the final say.

## Recent history: two men's absolute dominance

The Tour of Flanders over the past decade has been the exclusive territory of a handful of names. Since 2019, only four different riders have won: Alberto Bettiol in 2019, Van der Poel in 2020, 2022 and 2024, Pogačar in 2023 and 2025, and Kasper Asgreen in 2021. The common denominator among recent winners is the ability to produce power outputs exceeding 500 watts over two-to-five-minute efforts, exactly what the Kwaremont and Paterberg demand.

Van der Poel holds the record for six consecutive podiums at the Ronde (2020-2025), a mark no other rider has achieved in the race's modern history. Pogačar, for his part, has finished in the top three in every appearance. The rivalry between the two has elevated the race to extremes reminiscent of the Cancellara-Boonen era, when the Swiss and the Belgian traded victories with the regularity of a metronome.

The 110th edition has all the ingredients to be historic. A three-time champion chasing the record. A two-time champion who knows no limits. And a Belgian who has been waiting years for his turn at the most important race in his country. Antwerp to Oudenaarde, 271 kilometres, April 5. The rest is noise.
