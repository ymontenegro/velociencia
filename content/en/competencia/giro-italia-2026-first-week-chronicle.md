Of the 184 riders who left Nessebar on May 8, on the Bulgarian shore of the Black Sea, 177 made it to the first rest day. The 2026 Giro d'Italia — the 109th edition and the first ever Grande Partenza on Bulgarian soil — lost six riders in 48 hours, including three of the general classification leaders UAE Team Emirates-XRG had lined up for the overall fight. The maglia rosa that rests today on the shoulders of Guillermo Thomas Silva, a 24-year-old Uruguayan riding for XDS Astana Team, is as surprising as it is completely legitimate.

## Stage 1 | Nessebar → Burgas: Magnier Opens the Account

The opening stage ran 147 kilometres along the Black Sea coast without a single rated climb, with the main peloton averaging 43.85 km/h. Things got complicated 700 metres from the finish in Burgas when a mass crash split the front group and left sprinters scrambling for gaps that didn't exist. Paul Magnier, 22, of Soudal Quick-Step, emerged from the chaos with Jasper Stuyven leading him to the line, launched his sprint down the centre of the road and won in 3 hours, 21 minutes and 8 seconds. Tobias Lund Andresen (Decathlon CMA CGM) finished second; Ethan Vernon (NSN) took third. With the victory came the maglia rosa: Magnier became the third-youngest rider in history to wear it after the opening stage, behind only Wilfried Reybrouck in 1974 and Taylor Phinney in 2012. No French rider had led the Giro since Bruno Armirail in 2023.

## Stage 2 | Burgas → Veliko Tarnovo: Rain, Carnage and Thomas Silva's Miracle

The second stage changed the race entirely. The 221 kilometres to Veliko Tarnovo, the medieval capital of the second Bulgarian Empire, were ridden under persistent rain from the 50th kilometre onward. Wet asphalt turned every downhill bend into a gamble, and 21 kilometres from the finish the race was neutralised after a mass crash that brought down dozens of riders. The casualty list was immediate and serious. Jay Vine (UAE Team Emirates-XRG) was taken away in an ambulance with a broken elbow and concussion. His teammate Marc Soler suffered a fractured pelvis. Adam Yates crossed the finish with a torn jersey and dried blood on his face but didn't start the next morning, diagnosed with concussion. Santiago Buitrago, one of Antonio Tiberi's key mountain domestiques at Bahrain Victorious, also abandoned. Andrea Vendrame (Jayco AlUla) had three vertebral fractures and likewise didn't start stage 3. Six riders gone in the opening 48 hours of the Giro.

As the peloton reorganised after the neutralisation, Jonas Vingegaard chose the ascent to the Lyaskovets Monastery — the final obstacle before the finish — for the first serious GC move of the 2026 race. The Visma-Lease a Bike leader attacked with 12 kilometres to go and only Giulio Pellizzari (Red Bull-BORA-Hansgrohe) and Lennert Van Eetvelt (Lotto-Intermarché) could follow. The three built roughly 20 seconds on the chasing group. In the final kilometre, however, all three hesitated: none wanted to open the sprint and hand the stage to the other two. The delay was fatal. Jan Christen (UAE) bridged across from behind, the chasers closed the gap, and Guillermo Thomas Silva threaded through with the timing of a seasoned sprinter to take the win. He was the first Uruguayan rider to win a stage of the Giro d'Italia in 109 editions of the race. The maglia rosa passed from Magnier — dropped on the final climb — to Thomas Silva, who also pulled on the white jersey for best young rider.

## Stage 3 | Plovdiv → Sofia: Magnier Does It Again

The third stage closed the Bulgarian Grande Partenza with 175 kilometres from ancient Philippopolis to the capital. A breakaway threatened to hold on but the peloton closed it down five kilometres from the line. In the sprint, Jonathan Milan (Lidl-Trek) opened from a long way out; Magnier climbed up his back wheel and passed him in the final metres in a photo finish. Dylan Groenewegen (Unibet Rose Rockets) completed the podium in 4 hours, 9 minutes and 42 seconds. Two stage wins in three days: the first Frenchman in history to do it in the opening trio of a Giro. Magnier leads the maglia ciclamino points classification with 105 points, well ahead of Milan on 64. Thomas Silva kept the pink jersey without breaking a sweat.

## The GC Table Entering the Rest Day

Thomas Silva leads the overall standings with margins that mean almost nothing until the mountains arrive. Florian Stork (Tudor Pro Cycling) and Egan Bernal (Netcompany-Ineos) are four seconds back; Thymen Arensman, Bernal's teammate, and Giulio Ciccone (Lidl-Trek) sit at six. A group of 24 riders — including Jonas Vingegaard, Enric Mas, Lennert Van Eetvelt and Jan Christen — lies ten seconds from the lead. In practical terms, the GC race has not started.

## UAE Team Emirates: The Race's Hardest Hit

UAE Team Emirates-XRG arrived at the Giro with a powerful squad on paper. The crashes of the opening 48 hours dismantled it before the race saw its first real mountain. Adam Yates, the Briton designated as the team's GC leader in the absence of Pogačar and del Toro, didn't take the start of stage 3 after finishing Veliko Tarnovo with a shredded jersey and a confirmed concussion. Jay Vine, the team's time-trial specialist with his eyes on the Tuscany ITT, left the race on a stretcher with a broken elbow and concussion. Marc Soler, another climbing support rider, suffered a fractured pelvis. The team will ride into the Blockhaus — stage 7, the first genuine mountain finish with 246 kilometres and the final 10 km at 10% average gradient — with Jan Christen as their GC alternative. The 23-year-old Swiss has the talent for the role, but he was never supposed to play it. Racing has a way of creating leaders from gregarios.

## Vingegaard's Warning Shot

Vingegaard's first offensive move in stage 2 — described by international media as "Pogačar-like in its aggression" — produced no time gaps in the standings, but it sent a message the peloton won't be quick to forget. Visma-Lease a Bike DS Marc Reef put it plainly: "He showed that he is in excellent shape." Only Pellizzari and Van Eetvelt could follow on the climb. That no other GC contender could match his pace is information that matters far more in the Dolomites than it does in Bulgaria.

## Tuesday: Italy

On Tuesday, May 12, the peloton lands in Catanzaro at the tip of the Italian boot for the race's first stage on Italian soil. It's 138 kilometres to Cosenza with the Cozzo Tunno climb — 14.5 km at 5.9%, crested with 45 kilometres still to ride — as the only real obstacle before a 3.7% drag to the finish. It's a day for versatile sprinters, the kind of finish Tobias Lund Andresen favours. The GC won't move. But Tuesday begins the countdown to the Blockhaus, the first real filter that will tell us who can actually win in Rome on May 31.
