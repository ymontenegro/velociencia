## The problem isn't the list of rivals, it's Pogačar

Tadej Pogačar arrives at the 2026 Tour de France with a spring résumé that reads like an entire season for anyone else. Strade Bianche, Milan-San Remo, the Tour of Flanders and Liège-Bastogne-Liège all fell his way, and then he won the Tour de Suisse with crushing authority: 6:32 over Richard Carapaz after attacking on stage one. The Slovenian of UAE Emirates-XRG is chasing a fifth Tour, which would equal the all-time record held by Anquetil, Merckx, Hinault and Indurain.

The June question isn't whether Pogačar is the favourite, but whether anyone holds the specific weapon to hurt him at the three points where the 2026 route opens a crack. And this Tour, with just 45 kilometres against the clock —none of them long and flat— and eight high-mountain stages, is built for that fight to be decided uphill.

![Cyclist climbing a high-mountain pass above the clouds](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1506905925346-21bda4d32df4?w=900&q=75&fit=crop&crop=entropy&fm=webp&auto=format,compress)

What follows is not a stage-by-stage rundown of the route, but a dossier on the people. Who the challengers are, what weapon each one brings, and at which exact kilometres —the Vosges of stage 14, the time trial of stage 16, the double Alpe d'Huez of stages 19 and 20— each one can find his only window.

## Vingegaard: the only one who knows how to win, but he's coming off the Giro

Jonas Vingegaard is the only man in the peloton who has beaten Pogačar in a Grand Tour. He did it in 2022, when he ended the Slovenian's reign in the Alps, and repeated it in 2023 with one of the largest margins of the last decade: 7:29, sealed with a brutal time trial on stage 16 where he took 1:38. In five Tours, the Dane of Visma has never finished lower than second.

In 2026 he rewrote the script completely. He made his Giro d'Italia debut and won it with authority —five stages, all of them summit finishes, 5:22 over Felix Gall— to complete the set of all three Grand Tours and join the eight riders who have ever done it. The achievement is historic. The problem is the calendar.

The Giro-Tour double is the riskiest bet of his career. In the 21st century only one rider has pulled it off: Pogačar in 2024, inside his Triple Crown. Vingegaard had only a handful of recovery days before pivoting to the Tour, and the Alpine unknown is whether the kilometres of May will catch up with him exactly when the race is decided.

His weapon is sustained high mountain, the long climbs where a steady tempo breaks rivals. His best terrain in this Tour is stage 20, the queen stage of Saturday 25 July: Croix de Fer, Télégraphe, [the Galibier at 2,642 metres](/data/climbs/col-du-galibier-lautaret) and Sarenne before Alpe d'Huez, with 5,600 metres of accumulated climbing. There, if he arrives fresh, is where Vingegaard can turn the fatigue of an entire campaign into his favour. If he arrives empty, he will be the first to pay for it.

## Evenepoel: he bet the whole chip on freshness

Remco Evenepoel faces his first Tour with a new team. After leaving Soudal-QuickStep, the Belgian rides in 2026 in the colours of Red Bull-BORA-hansgrohe, where he shares leadership with Florian Lipowitz. His recent credential is emphatic: at the 2024 Tour de France he finished third overall, won the stage 7 time trial beating Pogačar by 12 seconds and took the white jersey for best young rider.

To that he adds a feat no man had achieved: the Olympic double gold in Paris 2024, winning both the road race and the time trial at the same Games. Evenepoel is the best pure time triallist among the GC contenders, and over three weeks that quality matters.

The problem is that 2026 deals him the worst possible hand for a time-trial specialist. With only 45 kilometres against the clock —concentrated in the lone 26 km individual TT of stage 16— the Tour minimises precisely where the Belgian is superior. His preparation confirms the strategy: he skipped the Giro and sat out the month of June, betting on altitude blocks and controlled training rather than racing rhythm. Red Bull-BORA prioritises freshness over kilometres in the legs.

The weakness is the same as ever. The long high mountain, the chained climbs above 2,000 metres, is where Pogačar and Vingegaard take time out of him. If Evenepoel wants the podium or higher, he needs to defend himself in the Vosges of stage 14 —around 4,000 metres of climbing but with shorter, more explosive ascents— and then bank everything possible in the stage 16 time trial between Évian and Thonon, which includes climbing and demands he limit his losses across the two days at Alpe d'Huez.

## Roglič: the podium as a realistic ceiling at 36

Primož Roglič, Evenepoel's teammate at Red Bull-BORA, comes to the 2026 Tour at 36 with the experience of a man who has won everything except this race. His role and the internal hierarchy of the team alongside Evenepoel and Lipowitz are the big tactical question for the Austrian outfit, and it will probably be settled on the road in the first summit finishes.

His June form was modest: he finished outside the top five at the Tour de Suisse, far from the Pogačar who steamrolled the race. At his age and in such a mountainous Tour, the podium is an ambitious but not far-fetched target for a rider of his consistency, provided health and crashes —his historic Achilles heel at the Tour— spare him.

Roglič has no weapon that frightens Pogačar, but he does have the race craft to slip into the fight for the honours places if the younger favourites falter. His best scenario is an attritional third week where veteran savvy outweighs the debutants.

## Del Toro: the apprentice who already wins, but enters as a domestique

Isaac del Toro is the most awkward story in this dossier, because his 2026 form would be enough to lead any other team. The 22-year-old Mexican won the UAE Tour, Tirreno-Adriatico and the Tour Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes —the former Critérium du Dauphiné— where he beat surprise runner-up Luke Tuckwell by 54 seconds, with Juan Ayuso third on the podium. In 2025 he was second on his Giro debut, losing the pink jersey only on stage 20 at the Finestre to Simon Yates, and became the second-youngest rider to stand on a Giro podium in history.

The catch is that del Toro rides for UAE Emirates-XRG, Pogačar's team. On his Tour debut he enters as a domestique and apprentice, not as a leader. His job is to protect the Slovenian in the mountains and, if Pogačar dominates comfortably, to aim for a podium spot without compromising the boss's strategy.

That subordination is his biggest sporting brake in 2026, but also his best schooling. If the Tour cracks open through a crash or a bad day for the leader, del Toro has the engine to reposition himself. His real GC chances will come when UAE stops having Pogačar as the absolute priority; in 2026 that won't happen, barring an accident.

## Seixas and Lipowitz: the wildcards of the new generation

Paul Seixas is the name nobody expected in the favourites' conversation. The Frenchman of Decathlon-CMA CGM won the Itzulia Basque Country and La Flèche Wallonne, and signed second places behind Pogačar at major races. He is the youngest rider in the Tour since the 1930s, which makes his debut a pure unknown: nobody knows how his body will respond in a third week. He abandoned the final stage of the Dauphiné after a crash, a reminder that fragility plays too.

Florian Lipowitz brings the opposite of Seixas: consistency without headlines. The German of Red Bull-BORA was third at the Volta a Catalunya, second at the Itzulia and the Tour de Romandie, and won the Tour of Slovenia. He shares leadership with Evenepoel, and several analyses rate him a more reliable bet than his teammate for a three-week GC precisely because of that consistency.

To them you add Matteo Jorgenson, the American of Visma who enters as the second tier of Vingegaard's team but with a top-10 ceiling if the race allows it. None of these three has the weapon to dethrone Pogačar today, but they can tangle up the podium and force UAE to spend domestiques ahead of schedule.

The route splits the opportunities into three acts. The Vosges of stage 14 favour the explosive riders and is the first serious test for Evenepoel, who must limit damage before his only real card. The 26 km time trial of stage 16, between Évian and Thonon and with climbing, is the day the Belgian can claw back seconds on the pure climbers, though 26 kilometres aren't enough to reverse a large gap.

The verdict is signed over the double [Alpe d'Huez](/data/climbs/alpe-dhuez) weekend. Stage 19 climbs the Col d'Ornon before the 13.8 kilometres at 8.1% of the 21 hairpins, and less than 24 hours later stage 20 chains Croix de Fer, Télégraphe, Galibier and Sarenne before returning to the mythical climb. There, in those 5,600 metres of climbing on Saturday 25 July, is where Vingegaard dreams that Pogačar's freshness —or his own— makes the difference. We break down each climb of the third week in the [analysis of the mountain stages and key climbs](/competition/tour-de-france-2026-mountain-stages-key-climbs-yellow-jersey); everything else is prologue.
