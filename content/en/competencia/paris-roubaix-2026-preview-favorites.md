258.3 kilometers separate Compiègne from the Roubaix velodrome. 30 cobbled sectors add up to 54.8 kilometers of pavé where a loose stone, a puncture or a single lapse in concentration can destroy months of preparation. The 123rd edition of the Hell of the North takes place this Sunday, April 12, and it arrives loaded with history waiting to be written. Mathieu van der Poel defends a throne he has held for three consecutive years and wants to equal the all-time record of four victories shared by Roger De Vlaeminck and Tom Boonen. Across the start line, Tadej Pogačar hunts the only Monument he has yet to win, the one that would make him the fourth cyclist in history to claim all five.

This is not just another race. It is the collision of two historic ambitions on the most brutal cobblestones on the calendar.

## A parcours that bites from the start

ASO's organizers have modified the first part of the route to make the race more selective from the opening kilometers. The detour eastward through the village of Briastre strings together the first four cobbled sectors with barely any tarmac between them. That density of pavé in the early going is unprecedented in recent editions, and the intent is clear: force teams to position early, punish those who sit too far back, and create selection before the decisive sectors arrive.

Sector 26, a rarely used stretch that includes an 800-meter climb over cobblestones, returns to the parcours after years of absence. That combination of gradient and pavé adds an ingredient that pure cobblestone specialists do not typically encounter at Roubaix, one that could favor riders with the ability to change pace uphill. The final 20 sectors, from the four-star Haveluy à Wallers to the Carrefour de l'Arbre, remain unchanged from 2025.

## The three stone judges

Three five-star sectors mark the boundaries between contenders and the rest. The Trouée d'Arenberg appears with 98 kilometers remaining: 2,400 meters in a straight line through a forest corridor where the cobbles are particularly rough and dust or mud — depending on the weather — can reduce visibility to almost nothing. Crashes here are frequent and their consequences final. Riders who exit Arenberg in trouble rarely make it back to the front.

Mons-en-Pévèle arrives later, when legs have already absorbed more than 200 kilometers of racing. Its 3,000 meters of loose, unpredictable pavé represent the point where differences in endurance start to outweigh tactics. The cobblestones shift beneath the wheels, bikes bounce uncontrollably, and holding a clean line demands brutal concentration. It is the sector that most often splits the race into two irreconcilable groups.

The Carrefour de l'Arbre closes the trilogy just 17 kilometers from the finish. 2,100 meters where countless editions have been decided. It is the last great stage for an attack before the velodrome, and riders who arrive with legs know it. This is where Van der Poel launched his winning move in 2023, where he consolidated his advantage in 2024, and where he stamped his authority again in 2025. If anyone wants to dethrone the Dutchman, they will likely need to do it here.

## Van der Poel: the cobblestone king chasing immortality

Mathieu van der Poel has won three consecutive editions of Paris-Roubaix, a feat not seen since Francesco Moser between 1978 and 1980. His statistics in this race defy logic: five starts, three victories, a 60% success rate in an event where even favorites abandon due to punctures or crashes with alarming regularity. No other active rider comes close to those numbers.

The Dutchman from Alpecin-Premier Tech arrives at this edition with the 2026 E3 Saxo Classic already in his palmares, his third consecutive triumph in that race. On that day he demonstrated that his level on cobblestones remains stratospheric: he attacked solo, resisted a four-man chase, and crossed the line with the same authority he displays at Roubaix. At Gent-Wevelgem he formed a spectacular two-man breakaway with Wout van Aert that was only neutralized with less than two kilometers to go.

A fourth victory would place him alongside Roger De Vlaeminck and Tom Boonen as the riders with the most wins in Paris-Roubaix history. Van der Poel is already a legend of this race, but equaling that record would make him the most dominant cobblestone rider the modern era has ever produced. His ability to absorb impacts while pedaling out of the saddle, his tactical reading of the sectors, and the depth of his team — with Jasper Philipsen, Florian Sénéchal and Oscar Riesebeek at his disposal — make him the clear favorite.

## Pogačar: the missing piece of the puzzle

Only three cyclists in 120 years of history have won all five Monuments: Rik Van Looy, Eddy Merckx, and Roger De Vlaeminck. Tadej Pogačar has four. He is missing Paris-Roubaix. The magnitude of what is at stake needs no embellishment: winning on Sunday would make him the fourth man to achieve it and, at 27 years old, the youngest on the list.

The Slovenian from UAE Team Emirates-XRG has already amassed 11 Monument victories, equaling De Vlaeminck in second place on the all-time list behind Merckx's 19. He has Milan-San Remo from two weeks ago, where he beat Pidcock after surviving a mass crash. He has three Liège-Bastogne-Liège titles, five Il Lombardia wins and two Tour of Flanders titles. But Roubaix is different. Roubaix demands skills that cannot be measured in watts per kilogram: instinct on cobblestones, mechanical risk management, the ability to stay calm when your bike bounces beneath you for kilometers on end.

In 2025, his debut at the race, Pogačar proved he can compete at Roubaix at the highest level. He matched every acceleration from Van der Poel until a misjudged turn on a cobbled section sent him into the barriers with 38 kilometers to go. He got up with his chain off, changed bikes, suffered an additional puncture and finished second, 1 minute and 18 seconds behind. Second on debut is an exceptional result, but the gap to Van der Poel was enormous.

This year he has completely overhauled his cobblestone setup. UAE has built his Colnago Y1Rs with Continental GP5000 tires at 35 millimeters, pushing the frame's tire capacity to its absolute limit. To create clearance for those wider tires, they eliminated the front derailleur entirely and he will race with a single chainring — a radical decision that shows how far he is willing to go. The handlebars feature double bar tape wrapping for vibration absorption. Every technical detail points to an obsession: never lose Roubaix to mechanical problems again.

Philippe Gilbert, former world champion and winner of four Monuments, stated that Pogačar "will never be the favorite for Roubaix" because the race demands a type of experience that cannot be improvised. He may be right. But writing off a rider who wins everything he sets his mind to would be the most dangerous mistake his rivals could make.

## Van Aert, Pedersen and the other hunters

Wout van Aert completes the top three of favorites. The Belgian from Visma-Lease a Bike has built a solid spring without spectacular headline results: third at Milan-San Remo after the crash he shared with Pogačar, and second at Dwars door Vlaanderen where Filippo Ganna caught him on the finish line after Van Aert had attacked solo. Those narrow defeats speak of a rider who has the legs but has not yet found his perfect day. Roubaix could be that day. His team includes Christophe Laporte — third at Gent-Wevelgem with consistent results all spring — Timo Kielich, Stefan Bissegger and Oliver Naesen, a lineup built for the cobblestones.

Mads Pedersen knows what it takes to reach the podium at Roubaix. The Dane from Lidl-Trek finished third in 2025 behind Van der Poel and Pogačar. His approach to Roubaix is surgical: meticulous effort distribution across 258 kilometers, never wasting a watt before the decisive sectors. He has shown consistency all spring with a fourth place at San Remo and arrives without the pressure of being a favorite but with the experience of knowing exactly what it takes to win.

Filippo Ganna burst into the conversation with his Dwars door Vlaanderen victory, where he destroyed Van Aert on the finish line after breaking his front wheel and handlebars in two separate incidents during the race. The Italian from INEOS Grenadiers has the raw power to dominate the pavé sectors — few cyclists in the peloton generate more watts — and his win over the Flemish terrain proved he can compete on the toughest ground. Matteo Jorgenson, who defended his Paris-Nice title, is another name to watch for his versatility and ability to stay at the front when the race gets hard.

## The spring that reveals the form

Results from the past three weeks provide a clear map of strengths. Pogačar won Milan-San Remo on March 21 in a finish decided by half a tire width against Pidcock, demonstrating exceptional form despite the crash he suffered before the Cipressa. Van der Poel conquered the E3 Saxo Classic on March 27 with a solo attack reminiscent of his best days, completing a hat-trick no other active rider can match.

Gent-Wevelgem on March 29 — now rebranded as In Flanders Fields — produced one of the most spectacular finishes of the season. Van der Poel and Van Aert escaped together with a gap that seemed decisive, but the peloton caught them with less than two kilometers to go and Jasper Philipsen took the sprint victory. That image encapsulates the dynamic of this spring: the big three have the legs to dominate, but neutralizing each other opens the door for third parties.

Dwars door Vlaanderen on April 1 closed the Flemish week with Ganna's surprise catch of Van Aert on the line. The Tour of Flanders takes place this Sunday, April 5 — one week before Roubaix — and will serve as the final form test for all contenders. Pogačar is the defending champion there, and Remco Evenepoel's confirmed debut adds an element of uncertainty that could alter everyone's tactics.

## The tactical battle ahead

The modified 2026 parcours favors early aggression. The succession of four sectors without respite at the start of the cobbled zone will force teams to burn domestiques earlier than usual, and riders who reach the Trouée d'Arenberg without team support will pay a heavy price. Alpecin-Premier Tech has the deepest roster for this kind of war of attrition. UAE has promised a strong team around Pogačar, but losing Jan Christen to the San Remo crash could leave a gap in their formation.

Weather will be another factor. Forecasts for the week ahead suggest dry conditions with temperatures around 12 degrees Celsius, which would favor a fast race with less risk of skidding but more dust in the sectors. In dry conditions, Van der Poel's technical skill reaches its peak expression. In the wet, chaos levels the playing field and opens possibilities for outsiders.

The most likely scenario involves progressive selection through the five-star sectors and a reduced group of three to five riders arriving at the Carrefour de l'Arbre. If Van der Poel attacks there, as he has done in the last three editions, the rest will need to follow or resign themselves to fighting for the podium. If Pogačar learns from his 2025 mistake and manages the risks without losing contact, the Roubaix velodrome could witness a two-man sprint that defines an era.

Two records at stake. One velodrome. 258 kilometers of hell to decide who writes their name in stone.
