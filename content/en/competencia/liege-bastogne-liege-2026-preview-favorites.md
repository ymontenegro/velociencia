259.5 kilometres and 4,395 metres of positive elevation gain. Eleven official climbs. A flat finish in Liège after descending from the Roche-aux-Faucons. The 112th edition of Liège-Bastogne-Liège takes place on Sunday 26 April and once again reclaims its status as the oldest Classic on the calendar, La Doyenne, the race Eddy Merckx won five times and where a new emperor is now trying to close in on that record.

Tadej Pogačar defends his title and chases a fourth Doyenne victory after winning in 2021, 2024 and 2025. The record is stark: four starts, only one failure. He will be facing Remco Evenepoel, a double winner in 2022 and 2023, alongside Tom Pidcock, Ben Healy, Mattias Skjelmose, Paul Seixas and a peloton that knows winning in Liège means ruling out first a rider who has already added two Monument victories to his palmarès this year.

## A route that respects tradition

Organisers have kept the classic structure and tweaked the middle section to add a few kilometres compared to 2025. The route leaves Liège, turns around in Bastogne, heads back north and links the eleven climbs that make up the Walloon Ardennes menu. The first one to appear is the Côte de Saint-Roch, a kilometre at 11.2%, which usually works as a hard warm-up after more than a hundred opening kilometres of rolling terrain.

Then come the Col de Haussire, the Côte de Wanne, the Côte de Stockeu —a kilometre at 12.5%— and the Côte de Haute-Levée, all linked within thirty kilometres. That triple trap marks the start of the real attrition. Next is the Col du Rosier, the longest climb of the day at 4.4 kilometres at 5.9%, followed by the Maquisard and the Côte de Desnié, short but accumulative ramps that prepare the ground for what comes next.

The Côte de la Redoute is the cardinal point of the race. One and a half kilometres at 9.4% average gradient, located just over thirty kilometres from the finish. It is the spot where Pogačar has launched decisive attacks in the past and where many teams build their entire strategy. The Côte des Forges acts as a transition. The Côte de la Roche-aux-Faucons, 1.3 kilometres at 11%, crowns the string of difficulties 13.3 kilometres from the end. From there, a technical descent and flat run-in to the finish in Liège.

## Pogačar: the year of absolutism

Tadej Pogačar arrives at La Doyenne with a spring already in the record books. He won Strade Bianche, took Milan-San Remo after edging Tom Pidcock on the Via Roma and delivered an unforgettable performance at the Tour of Flanders with an attack on the Oude Kwaremont that shattered Van der Poel and Evenepoel and took him solo into Oudenaarde. At Paris-Roubaix he could not complete the set and finished second behind Wout van Aert, but simply being in the fight for the Hell of the North on wet cobbles confirmed how far he has stretched his range.

The tactical read is obvious. Pogačar needs the race to break on the Redoute or the Roche-aux-Faucons to exploit terrain where his power-to-weight ratio lets him open gaps the peloton cannot close. UAE Team Emirates-XRG have shown it at every spring appointment: they toughen the race from far out, wear down rival squads and leave the Slovenian in a position where a single attack closes the day.

Four wins at La Doyenne would place him just one behind Merckx, author of five victories between 1969 and 1975. No active rider is anywhere near that number. Winning on Sunday would add another Monument to a palmarès that already ranks among the most extensive in modern cycling history.

## Evenepoel: the man who knows how to win in Liège

Remco Evenepoel arrives at La Doyenne with two histories behind him. He won in 2022, when it became his first-ever Monument, and repeated in 2023 with an attack from the Roche-aux-Faucons that nobody could follow. This year, his plan goes first through the Amstel Gold Race on Sunday 19 April, where he seeks revenge after losing to Mattias Skjelmose in a three-up sprint in 2025. The Amstel result will give the first real clue about his form before La Flèche Wallonne on Wednesday 22 and La Doyenne.

His spring has had more light than shadow. He finished third at the Tour of Flanders, a noteworthy result for a debutant in a race that does not match his natural profile. Evenepoel acknowledged afterwards that Pogačar simply would not let him come back after the Kwaremont attack, but he left clear signals: the legs are there. Soudal Quick-Step have built a roster designed to protect him until the Roche-aux-Faucons and give him the launch he needs to repeat what he did in 2023.

His historical strength in Liège is no coincidence. The race rewards riders capable of launching a long attack, holding high wattage for ten minutes and closing out a finish against the clock. That is exactly Evenepoel's profile.

## The outsiders who could flip the script

Tom Pidcock was runner-up at Milan-San Remo and arrives feeling good after a steady spring. His ability to survive in selective finales and his top-five history at La Doyenne make him the most credible outsider. If the race ends in a reduced bunch, his explosiveness is a problem for everyone.

Ben Healy was third at the 2025 Doyenne and comes in again in good form. The Irishman of EF Education-EasyPost is the archetypal long-range attacker, always ready to exploit gaps that open when the big teams look at each other. The Redoute could be his stage.

Mattias Skjelmose takes over leadership at Lidl-Trek after Juan Ayuso was ruled out of the Ardennes due to a viral infection detected after the Itzulia. The Dane arrives as defending Amstel champion and with a profile that also works on the longer races. Giulio Ciccone, second at the 2025 Doyenne, completes the American team's hand.

Paul Seixas is the big French card. The 19-year-old from Decathlon CMA CGM has built the entire first half of his season targeting Liège directly, skipping the Amstel to arrive fresher. His progression in 2026 has been rapid and the team believes he can fight for the podium on a course that suits his qualities. Kévin Vauquelin rides for Arkéa-B&B Hotels after a solid spring that keeps him on the second-tier radar.

## Sunday's tactics

The big question is not who attacks, but where. The Redoute arrives 34 kilometres from the finish and allows long-range moves that require a pact between several riders to reach the line together. The Roche-aux-Faucons, by contrast, leaves only thirteen kilometres to manage the gap and favours riders with pure explosiveness on the final metres of a demanding climb.

Pogačar can afford to wait for the Roche-aux-Faucons because his time-trialling is exceptional. Evenepoel, instead, usually benefits from launching from further out to choke rivals with a pace they cannot match. Healy and Seixas need the Redoute or earlier. Pidcock and Skjelmose, with fast finishes, prefer a reduced group reaching the final descent.

Liège does not always follow the script. In 2011, Philippe Gilbert won with an attack twenty kilometres from the finish. In 2020, Primož Roglič beat Pogačar in a sprint by millimetres. La Doyenne has the power to turn each edition into a different story. On Sunday, the man to beat is still the same rider who has just won the Tour of Flanders and Milan-San Remo in barely three weeks. But he is carrying a long calendar on his shoulders and, for the first time this spring, he has just lost one.
