## Eighty Kilometers Alone in Tuscany to Open the Season

On March 7, 2026, Tadej Pogačar attacked 80 kilometers from the finish at Strade Bianche and arrived alone at Piazza del Campo in Siena. No rider in the history of the Tuscan classic had ever won it four times; Pogačar did it with an exhibition that Isaac del Toro and the young Frenchman Paul Seixas could only observe from a distance. It was his first race of the year, his season debut, and the manner in which he chose to introduce himself to the cycling world in 2026 was entirely consistent with how he has operated for the past five seasons. The Strade Bianche sent the signal that the level of 2026 would not fall below any previous year.

The race had unfolded normally for 180 kilometers. On the most demanding gravel sector, with the peloton fragmented and the dry Tuscan dust of a rain-free morning filling the air, Pogačar opened a gap that would never close. The distance at which he attacked was absurd by conventional standards, but Pogačar has spent years operating in a framework where conventional standards do not apply. Del Toro and Seixas completed the podium well behind. The UAE Team Emirates XRG Slovenian began the spring with the outright Strade Bianche record in his pocket.

## What Is at Stake in 2026

Four Tours de France, four Il Lombardias, four Liège-Bastogne-Lièges, and four Strade Bianche victories form the foundation from which Pogačar faces 2026. He is only 27 years old and already holds more monument wins than most riders accumulate in an entire career. The declared objective of the season is a fifth Tour de France, which would place him alongside Jacques Anquetil, Eddy Merckx, Bernard Hinault, and Miguel Induráin in the most exclusive club in road cycling history. He also wanted to complete the full set of five classic monuments with a victory that had eluded him until 2026: Milan-San Remo.

The calendar Pogačar designed for this year is, by any conventional measure, impossible. Strade Bianche, Milan-San Remo, Tour of Flanders, Paris-Roubaix, and both Ardennes classics, then the Tour de Romandie and Tour de Suisse as Tour de France preparation. In previous seasons, Pogačar had concentrated his spring on Italian monuments and the Ardennes, avoiding the cobblestoned roads of Paris-Roubaix. For 2026, the Slovenian decided to complete the cycle. What followed was the most dominant spring performance European professional cycling has witnessed in decades.

## San Remo After the Crash

The Milan-San Remo of March 21, 2026, was the hardest-earned of Pogačar's victories this season. On the Cipressa climb, with the race fractured into groups, Pogačar crashed and was momentarily outside contention. The group of favorites was rolling at 50 kilometers per hour; recovering was a task that in any other rider would have been effectively impossible. Pogačar returned to the leading group before the Poggio, attacked on the descent, and arrived at the Via Roma finish line after 298 kilometers — 6 hours, 35 minutes, and 49 seconds of racing — to win the sprint against Tom Pidcock. Wout van Aert completed the podium. It was the first time Pogačar had won La Classicissima, the one major monument that had been missing from his palmarès.

The tactical merit of the San Remo victory goes beyond physical capacity. Managing a crash on the Cipressa with 45 kilometers remaining, staying calm during the pursuit, and arriving at the sprint with enough left to beat Pidcock requires a mental control that no training bloc generates on its own. The combined result of Strade Bianche plus San Remo in two weeks set a tone for the spring that was not going to moderate itself.

## Flanders and the First Great Battle of the Spring

The Tour of Flanders on April 5 delivered the duel the spring had been promising: Pogačar against Mathieu van der Poel and Remco Evenepoel on the final ascent of the Oude Kwaremont. Pogačar attacked first and attacked decisively, opening a gap that neither Van der Poel nor Evenepoel could close before Oudenaarde. It was his third victory at the Ronde van Vlaanderen, tying the race's historical win record. Evenepoel finished third in his first participation in De Ronde, adding another notable milestone to the Belgian cycling collective's 2026 performance.

One week later, on April 13, Paris-Roubaix brought Pogačar to the cobblestones for the first time in his career. The race was a chaos of punctures and bike changes for the favorites: both Pogačar and Van der Poel suffered mechanical problems and had to chase back. At the Roubaix velodrome, Wout van Aert won the sprint over Pogačar by a wheel's width. It was the only one-day race of the spring that escaped the Slovenian. The podium was Van Aert-Pogačar-Stuyven, with Van der Poel fourth. The defeat, as narrow as it was, stands as the single entry in the losses column of a spring that has no modern parallel.

## Ardennes: The Fourth Doyenne

The Ardennes week brought news that shaped the season's narrative. Pogačar chose to skip La Flèche Wallonne — focusing all preparation on Liège-Bastogne-Liège — and the decision proved correct: with both Pogačar and Evenepoel absent from the Mur de Huy, 19-year-old Paul Seixas won his first WorldTour classic, outpacing Mauro Schmid and Ben Tulett in a performance that confirmed him as the revelation of the 2026 spring. Four days later, Liège-Bastogne-Liège completed the picture with one of the most dominant results of Pogačar's career on the Belgian roads.

The fourth Liège victory came with an attack on the Côte de la Roche-aux-Faucons, the same classic inflection point of the Belgian monument. The 45-second gap he maintained over Paul Seixas at the finish in Liège does not fully capture how quickly the decision was made: when Pogačar accelerated, the race was over for everyone else in that moment. Evenepoel was third, collecting an Ardennes podium in a week that would have been extraordinary for any other rider. The fourth Doyenne matches his victory count at Strade Bianche: two classics in which he holds the outright all-time win record, plus the first Milan-San Remo earned in 2026.

## Tour de Romandie: Nine Wins in Three Months

From April 28 to May 3, Pogačar made his Tour de Romandie debut and won it within the first four days through four stage victories. It was his first appearance at the Swiss race and he approached it the same way he approaches every new challenge: attacking without reserve. The overall victory came with a 42-second margin over Florian Lipowitz and 2 minutes 44 seconds over Lenny Martinez. His total victory count for 2026 rose to nine after Romandie: four monuments won (five entered, with Roubaix a second place), four Swiss stages, and the overall classification.

The choice of Romandie as Tour de France preparation reflects a deliberate strategic decision. For 2026, Pogačar passed on the Tour Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes — the race known until this year as the Critérium du Dauphiné, whose final edition under that name he won himself — and opted instead to combine Romandie (late April) with the Tour de Suisse (June 14-28) as his preparation block. Both are one-week Alpine races that allow him to fine-tune form without the 21-day attrition of a full Grand Tour. The Tour de Suisse will be the final examination before the Tour de France in July.

## UAE Team Emirates XRG: The World's Most Complete Team

UAE Team Emirates XRG enters the 2026 Tour de France as the team with the most victories of the season by a wide margin. Isaac del Toro, who finished on the Strade Bianche podium and leads UAE at the 2026 Giro d'Italia as the team's candidate for the Italian Grand Tour, represents the depth that gives Pogačar an unparalleled support structure for July. The team can present itself at the Tour with Pogačar as an uncontested leader and a domestique structure that no other World Tour team can match in quality or depth.

The team context also matters for understanding Pogačar's tactical approach in July. UAE has sufficient depth to control the Tour's peloton pace if the primary leader does not need to take risky early moves. The tactical question for the Tour will be whether Jonas Vingegaard and Visma-Lease a Bike, or Remco Evenepoel and Soudal-Quick Step, have enough collective engine power to force the race before the final passes — by which point Pogačar will typically have won the time trial and established gaps on the medium-difficulty finishes.

## The Fifth Tour and the Hall of Five

Four Tour de France victories — won in 2020, 2021, 2024, and 2025 — already place Pogačar as the only living cyclist who can enter the club of five-time Tour champions. Jacques Anquetil, Eddy Merckx, Bernard Hinault, and Miguel Induráin are the four names in that group; none of them won their five Tours in the 21st century, and none of them accumulated a spring classics season with the density of victories that Pogačar has built in 2026. A fifth Tour would not merely be the entry into a historical elite — it would be the confirmation that cycling in the first quarter of the 21st century produced the most complete rider the sport has ever seen.

The Tour de France 2026 will begin in July. Pogačar will arrive having won nine races in three months, having stood on the podium at all six spring monuments he entered, having lost only one — Paris-Roubaix — and that one in a velodrome sprint against the best cobbled classics sprinter of the year. Vingegaard, his historical rival, will have spent the year building his form for July. Evenepoel will want to demonstrate that two Ardennes third places represent his floor, not his ceiling. The narrative is in place. Pogačar's form, at the close of this report, is that of a rider who has not found a limit in 2026.

## Paul Seixas and the New Rival Pogačar Needed

One detail of the 2026 spring that deserves its own paragraph is the emergence of Paul Seixas as the rider who challenged Pogačar most consistently across the classics. The 19-year-old Frenchman finished on the Strade Bianche podium, placed second at Liège-Bastogne-Liège 45 seconds behind, and at La Flèche Wallonne — with Pogačar and Evenepoel absent — won his first WorldTour classic on the Mur de Huy. His climbing has a technical purity and an acceleration capacity that recalls the best young riders of the past two decades. After Liège, Pogačar acknowledged that Seixas was the rider who demanded the most from him throughout the entire spring — when Pogačar offers that kind of recognition, the cycling community takes note.

Seixas will almost certainly not beat Pogačar in 2026. But what matters for the future of the sport is that a 19-year-old exists who can challenge him on the hardest finishes of the season, and that rider arrives at the Tour de France 2026 with a spring résumé that justifies including him in any podium conversation. For Pogačar, the existence of a rival capable of pushing him in the mountains is part of what sustains the racing level we have witnessed this year. The Tour in July will tell us whether the spring was a preview of what is possible, or whether the ceiling is higher still.
