Isaac del Toro tops the 2026 UCI calendar-year ranking with a lead of close to 800 points over the rest of the peloton, according to the count reported by *CyclingUpToDate*. At 22, the Mexican rider of UAE Team Emirates-XRG is the cyclist who has performed best so far this season. The figure sums up a start to the year that no Latin American had ever put together so young. And the underlying detail is that he built that haul as team leader, not as a domestique.

## The year's number one, with a nuance worth reading

Del Toro dominates the calendar-year ranking, but the picture changes depending on which standings you look at. In the 52-week UCI WorldTour ranking — the one that blends the previous season with the current one — Tadej Pogačar led with 11,355 points, followed by Del Toro himself with 7,199 and Jonas Vingegaard with 6,369, according to *CyclingUpToDate*'s tally from 19 March 2026. In other words: across the 2026 calendar nobody has scored more than the Mexican, while in the rolling window he is still paying the price for not being Pogačar.

| UCI WorldTour 52-week ranking (as of 19 March 2026) | Rider | Points |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | Tadej Pogačar | 11,355 |
| 2nd | Isaac del Toro | 7,199 |
| 3rd | Jonas Vingegaard | 6,369 |

That nuance does not diminish the achievement, it contextualizes it. Leading the calendar year means that, since January, Del Toro has won or scored more than anyone, including the world champion. The gap to Pogačar in the one-year ranking is explained by the machine of victories the Slovenian carries over from 2025. For a rider who has not yet completed a full season as a leader, sitting second in that standing and first in the year's is a statement of intent.

## From Ensenada to the Giro podium

Del Toro was born on 27 November 2003 in Ensenada, Baja California, and his leap to the front rank came at the 2025 Giro d'Italia. That year he took the maglia rosa after the gravel-sector stage and defended it for several days, until Simon Yates snatched it away on the penultimate stage. He finished runner-up in a Grand Tour at just 21. It was confirmation that the Mexican was not a prospect but a contender.

That second place changed the conversation around Del Toro. He went from being UAE's precocious domestique to a rider with arguments to win three weeks. The question stopped being whether he could fight for a Grand Tour and became when he would do it as outright leader. The 2026 season began answering that question race after race.

## The 2026 harvest

Del Toro recorded his first UCI WorldTour general classification wins in 2026: the UAE Tour and Tirreno-Adriatico. At the UAE Tour he took two stages and the overall, according to *Cyclingnews*, doing so while breaking the youngest-winner record held by his own teammate, Tadej Pogačar. He settled the first of those stages with a late attack on a straight with a slight rise, surprising the sprinters and puncheurs who were expecting a bunch finish. Winning that way, stealing the race from the fast men, defines the kind of rider he is.

Tirreno-Adriatico completed the picture and made clear the UAE Tour had been no fluke. Two WorldTour stage races within a few weeks, both as team leader, established Del Toro as the most consistent rider of the early season. On top of that base came a collection of one-day wins that inflated his UCI points total. The result is that calendar-year lead of nearly 800 units.

## The Dauphiné as June's test

Del Toro is among the announced favorites for the Critérium du Dauphiné, held from 7 to 14 June. The French race is, year after year, the last major dress rehearsal before the Tour, and it gathers much of the July GC field over a week of pure climbing. For the Mexican it is the chance to measure himself at altitude against a roster of heavyweights before the summer. It is, in practice, the exam that will say whether his calendar-year dominance holds up when the road really tilts upward.

The Dauphiné will also help gauge where Del Toro stands against the new generation pushing up from below. The race preview places him fighting with names such as Juan Ayuso, João Almeida, Mattias Skjelmose and the French prodigy Paul Seixas. That pack of young climbers is the one that will define the Grand Tours of the coming years. June will say who arrives best at July.

## The paradox: number one of the year and a domestique at the Tour

Del Toro will debut at the 2026 Tour de France, but not as a leader: he will ride in support of Tadej Pogačar, as he confirmed himself to *Cyclingnews*. The rider who tops the calendar-year ranking will subordinate himself to the world champion, who in July will go for his fifth Tour title. It is the paradox that defines the Mexican's situation within UAE: he is the world's second-best rider by the 2026 calendar and, even so, the second man of his own team. That internal hierarchy sets his ceiling in the short term.

He framed the appointment as part of a plan, not as an imposed sacrifice. Learning the Tour from the inside, alongside the best rider on the planet, is a schooling few young riders receive. The reasonable doubt is how long a rider capable of winning Grand Tours on his own will accept that role. Recent cycling history is full of domestiques who ended up looking for air at other teams.

## What's at stake

Del Toro reaches the midpoint of 2026 as the biggest Latin American breakthrough since Egan Bernal himself. His calendar-year lead, his two WorldTour overalls and his 2025 Giro runner-up spot sketch a rider who is no longer a project but a reality. The Dauphiné will measure his ceiling in the mountains and the Tour will measure his patience as the domestique of a champion who has no intention of handing over command. Between those two tests lies the question of whether 2026 was his launch year or the first of several.

For Mexico and for the region, the moment is hard to overstate. Never had a Mexican rider led the world's annual cycling standings or installed himself so early among the three-week contenders. Del Toro turned a country with no Grand Tour tradition into a fixture the peloton has to reckon with. The question is no longer whether he will win a Grand Tour, but when and under which jersey he will contest it as the outright master of his team.
