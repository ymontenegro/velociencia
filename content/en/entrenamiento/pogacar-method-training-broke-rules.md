## The day the textbook stopped working

Thirty-nine minutes and forty-three seconds. That is how long Tadej Pogačar took on July 14, 2024, to climb the 15.8 kilometers of Plateau de Beille on stage 15 of the Tour de France. He did it at a vertical ascent rate of 1887 meters per hour and at an estimated 6.90 watts per kilogram, according to the reconstructions Lanterne Rouge published after the stage. Three minutes and forty-five seconds faster than Marco Pantani's 1998 benchmark of 43:28, set at the peak of the EPO era.

The number would be strange on its own. What makes it genuinely uncomfortable for contemporary training orthodoxy is that Pogačar reached that point after 200 kilometers and 5,000 meters of climbing that day, and in a season in which he had already won the Giro a few weeks earlier. He did not just climb faster than anyone on record at the Tour; he did it inside a calendar that no modern exercise physiologist would have signed off on.

That is the methodological black hole around the Slovenian. Pogačar trains and races as if the accepted evidence on periodization, intensity distribution and the limits of double peaking did not apply to him. So far, he has not been wrong.

## The San Millán years: five seasons on zone 2

For his first five years at UAE Team Emirates, from 2019 to 2023, Pogačar worked with Iñigo San Millán, the Spanish exercise physiologist and professor at the University of Colorado. San Millán's model, extensively described in his long-running podcast appearances with Peter Attia, is built on the same polarized principle popularized by Stephen Seiler and the Scandinavian school: roughly 80% of sessions in zone 2, understood as the highest intensity at which blood lactate remains close to 2 mmol/L and fat oxidation is still the dominant fuel.

San Millán has said publicly that the first thing that caught his attention about the young Slovenian was his unusually low lactate values for the power he was producing, an indirect marker of mitochondrial efficiency that became the methodological obsession of the program. The goal, as he has explained in interviews with Peter Attia and on INSCYD, was to maximize lactate recycling and build the mitochondrial density needed to sustain high power outputs while burning a large proportion of fat. Three to four long zone 2 sessions per week, lasting one to three hours each, formed the backbone.

During that period Pogačar won two Tours, a Liège, a Lombardia, a Flanders, and stacked podiums. Judged on results alone, the method worked. But 2023 introduced a caveat: Jonas Vingegaard and Visma beat him clearly on the longest Tour climbs, and Pogačar himself started talking privately about stagnation.

## 2024: enter Javier Sola, the emphasis shifts

The pivot came in October 2023. San Millán accepted the role of head of high performance at Athletic Club de Bilbao and, while he kept a consulting arrangement with UAE under a deal between the two clubs, Pogačar's day-to-day programming passed to Javier Sola, an in-house UAE coach already working with riders such as Adam Yates. Asked by Velo, the Slovenian explained it without dressing it up: "After five years or so with Iñigo, it was a little bit much with the same training." Performance director Jeroen Swart added the sentence that frames the new philosophy: "We looked at everything we could optimize — training structures, tires, pressures, bike positions, even rim widths."

Sola did not throw zone 2 out, but he moved it off the top of the menu. In its place he added more threshold work and, crucially, more VO2max and fast-twitch intervals, according to descriptions from Velo and Cyclingnews. The pyramid widened at the top: 30/30 sets, 4x8-minute blocks above FTP, and extended tempo tailored to the Tour's HC climbs. Pogačar went on to win the 2024 Giro by nearly ten minutes over Daniel Martínez, claim yellow in Paris six weeks later, and close the season with the world championship in Zurich.

## The numbers that actually check out

The most-cited study of his physiology is by Norwegian professor Ole Kristian Berg, published in the Journal of Science and Cycling. Berg used public data — 176 cm, 66 kg, bike weight, climbing times — to back out critical power across six HC climbs at the 2024 Tour. The conclusion: an average output around 442 watts sustained for about forty minutes, ranges of 6.4 to 7 W/kg, and an estimated VO2max close to 96 mL/kg/min after applying the classical critical-power-to-VO2max conversion.

That number needs careful reading. Berg derives it from races rather than lab tests, and Pogačar himself told Cyclist in early 2026 that he does not remember the last time someone directly measured his VO2max. What it does, though, is place him at the upper end of the known human range. The table below summarizes the three public climbing performances most often referenced in specialized press.

## Altitude, heat and the weight room

Altitude: in 2024 Pogačar spent three weeks at Isola 2000 before the Tour, following the Mathieu van der Poel approach of going from elevation straight into the race without intermediate tune-up events. In 2025 he repeated the format at Sierra Nevada, where Cyclingnews reported he overlapped with Vingegaard himself at the same resort, the two rivals housed in separate blocks a short walk apart. A video from that block showed him climbing a 6% ramp alone at 2,200 meters.

Heat acclimation: Swart described to Velo a protocol of three heat sessions per week for six weeks before the Tour, with internal temperature monitoring. According to the figures he cited, that volume produces roughly a 4% increase in hemoglobin mass and a 20 to 25% expansion of plasma volume. Pogačar has posted photos of himself training indoors in full winter clothing in the middle of a Monaco summer.

Strength: Alexandre Baccili, a strength and conditioning specialist in Monaco, runs the gym side of the program. Sola called it a "fundamental pillar" of the step forward Pogačar took in 2025. The focus, Baccili has said, is closing gaps in peak torque production — specifically the big-ring seated accelerations on climbs that Pogačar was already using in 2022 and that have become his tactical signature.

## The calendar sin

The other rule the method breaks sits in the race calendar. In 2024 he rode Giro, Tour and Worlds, and won all three. In 2025 he added Flanders and Liège before the Tour and defended his world title in October. For 2026 UAE announced a schedule that includes all five Monuments — Milan-San Remo, Flanders, Roubaix, Liège and Lombardia — plus Strade Bianche, Romandie, Switzerland, the Tour and Worlds. Nobody in the modern era has contested the five Monuments in a single season; UAE general manager Joxean Matxin framed the choice in terms of motivation and stimulus variety.

The contrast with Visma is blunt. Vingegaard, with his team, follows a more conservative blueprint: long blocks at Teide and Tignes, thirty or more weekly hours in construction phase, and a single summer peak. The model sits closer to the polarized canon Seiler described back in 2010. Pogačar, by contrast, layers high volume, VO2max loading and continuous racing exposure, something the intensity distribution literature — Filipas et al. (2022) and onward — would classify as a variable pyramid rather than pure polarized work.

## The three doubts that remain

Three honest doubts remain on the table. The first is physiological: no Grand Tour rider has sustained this profile across this many consecutive seasons without a dip, and Pogačar has not taken an extended break since 2020. The second is methodological: how much of "the method" is actually replicable, and how much of the output sits on a genetic ceiling that Berg places against the known human limit. The third is competitive: riding five Monuments, a Tour and a World Championship in twelve months demands a load tolerance no rider has demonstrated over a full career.

Matxin insists that, at twenty-seven, Pogačar is still "at 70% of his potential". Taken literally the claim has no physiological basis. But it captures what the method actually stands for — a willingness to move the piece the training manual says should stay put, and to check the balance afterwards. As of April 2026, the ledger is still firmly in the Slovenian's favor.
